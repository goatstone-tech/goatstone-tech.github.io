Data Destruction

Sanitization following NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2, with verification, validation, and a certificate for every drive.

The three methods (NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2)

Clear: applies logical techniques to sanitize data in all user-addressable storage locations, protecting against simple non-invasive recovery. It is done with software, and is picked by an organization when a device stays with the same owner or inside the same organization. You can ask us to do this! But it isn't free, even if you keep the items.

Purge: applies physical or logical techniques that make recovery infeasible even with the latest forensic laboratory methods, while keeping the device usable. We mainly use firmware commands described in IEEE 2883-2022 that are the standard route for drives that will be resold, or that simply won't go back to your organization.

Destroy: What it sounds like. For whatever can't be cleared or purged, we make sure recovery is infeasible, and the device is unusable. The most destructive methods are implemented by our downstream partners; disintegration or incineration.

Verification and validation

We run 2 checks on every job.

Verification: we read the tool result, the error status, and the drive health data. That way we ensure the method we chose worked!

Validation: One of our technicians reviews those results and signs it as accepted or rejected. So, a completed test isn't automatically a job well done. A rejected job gets re-run with a different technique or moved up to a stronger method. Then, we certify the job for you.

Your certificate

Each destroyed or purged drive receives a certificate documenting the required information under NIST Rev. 2, Section 4.6, including:

Drive details: Manufacturer, model, serial number, media type, and source.

Sanitization details: Method, technique, tool, and tool version.

Verification: Verification method used.

Validation records: Validator's name, title, date, location, and signature.

About "DoD wipes"

If you know about ITAD, you have heard of DoD.

Why don't we use that standard? Well, yes, some vendors still advertise multi-pass "DoD 5220.22-M" wipes, but that standard's overwrite specifications were removed by the DoD itself in 2006. Yeah, long time. Therefore, multi-pass overwriting is the wrong tool for modern drives, especially SSDs with overprovisioned space a basic overwrite cannot reach. The current federal guidance is provided by NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2, and that is what we will follow.

Data Destruction FAQ

Overwrite or firmware erase: which do my drives get?

We use validated Purge methods for SSDs and NVMe drives, such as firmware-based sanitization or Cryptographic Erase (CE), depending on the drive's capabilities. We avoid relying on multi-pass overwriting for these drives because it may leave data behind in inaccessible flash memory cells and, well, you don't want that.

For HDDs that won't go back to you, we use appropriate, validated Purge methods to help ensure the data cannot be recovered. If a drive is staying with you for reuse, we may use Clear when the data sensitivity and security requirements allow it, and per your preference.

What if a drive fails or is locked?

It simply escalates! We will use a different technique, but if we can't use software, then we proceed with Destroy. The certificate documents what actually happened so you always know where the device went and where the data didn't.
